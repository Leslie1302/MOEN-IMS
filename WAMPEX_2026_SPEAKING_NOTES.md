# WAMPEX 2026 — Speaking Notes
## Hon. Dr. John Abdulai Jinapor (MP), Minister for Energy and Green Transition
### Keynote Address: Energy Solutions for the Mining Industry
**Date:** 3–5 June 2026 | **Venue:** La Palm Royal Beach Hotel, Accra

---

Good morning.

Mining does not run on ore alone. It runs on power.

And the truth is, for years, the mining sector in West Africa has had to navigate an energy landscape that was unreliable, unpredictable, and at times, inadequate. That is not a new observation. But it is one that demands urgent action.

Ghana's power sector has been quite checkered. I have been deputy minister before. I have virtually acted before in this sector. And now I am a substantive minister. So I understand the enormity of the challenge — not in theory, but from experience.

When we assumed office in January 2025, we inherited a sector in visible distress. GRIDCo's records for 2024 showed persistent load shedding, culminating in a shortfall of over 700 megawatts in December 2024 alone. Every single month that year, there was load shedding. That is the reality we walked into.

For the mining industry, that unreliability is not an inconvenience. It is a direct cost. Diesel generators fill the gap when the grid cannot. That cost gets passed along the value chain.

We made a commitment. And we have delivered.

For several months now, Ghana has maintained a stable power supply, with no load shedding. We are not just meeting domestic demand. Ghana is exporting power to Burkina Faso, Côte d'Ivoire, and Togo. That is not a statement of ambition. That is a fact.

And so the question for this gathering is not whether Ghana can power its mines. It is how we build an energy architecture that matches the scale of your ambitions.

We are working on three fronts.

First, grid reliability. We are investing in transmission and distribution infrastructure to ensure mining regions have consistent, quality power. GRIDCo and VRA are coordinating on reinforcements targeted at high-demand mining zones.

Second, diversification. Not every mine is connected to the national grid, and not every mine should wait for a connection. We are opening the space for independent power producers, solar-hybrid solutions, and embedded generation tailored to mining operations. The Energy Commission is finalising frameworks to make this easier, not harder.

Third, the green transition. Mining is energy-intensive. The global demand for accountability on emissions is real and growing. We are not asking you to choose between fossil fuels and renewables. We are integrating both strategically to power growth, jobs, and resilience — while reducing the carbon footprint of extraction.

Africa has every reason to lead in this space. We have the minerals the world needs. We have the solar resource. What we need is the policy certainty, the infrastructure, and the partnerships to bring them together.

This is not about extracting more. It is about extracting smarter, powering better, and ensuring that the wealth generated from our resources stays on this continent.

Ghana is ready to lead that conversation. We are not looking for passengers. We are looking for partners.

I am happy to take your questions.

Thank you.
